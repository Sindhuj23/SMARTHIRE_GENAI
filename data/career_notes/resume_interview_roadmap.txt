SKILL ROADMAP: BUILDING A STRONG TECH RESUME AND INTERVIEW PROFILE

Resume Fundamentals:
- Keep it to one page for entry-level and early-career roles.
- Every bullet point should follow: Action verb + what you did + measurable result, where possible.
  Weak: "Worked on a dashboard project."
  Strong: "Built a Power BI dashboard that reduced weekly reporting time by 30%."
- List skills that match the job description's language exactly (many companies use automated resume screening that looks for keyword matches).
- Put your most relevant/recent project or experience first, not chronologically buried.

Building a Portfolio (for Data/AI/ML roles):
- 2-3 solid projects beat 10 shallow ones. Depth matters more than quantity.
- Every project should have a public GitHub repo with a clear README: problem statement, approach, tools used, results, and what you'd improve.
- Deploy at least one project so it's clickable, not just code (Streamlit Cloud, Hugging Face Spaces, or similar).
- Include a mix: one that shows technical depth (e.g. a model you built), and one that shows end-to-end thinking (e.g. a deployed app).

General Interview Preparation:
- Prepare a 60-90 second "walk me through your background" answer — this gets asked almost everywhere.
- For technical roles, expect a mix of: coding/technical questions, a project deep-dive, and behavioral questions.
- Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral questions.
- Always prepare 2-3 thoughtful questions to ask the interviewer at the end.

Common Mistakes Across All Roles:
- Applying with a generic resume that isn't tailored to the specific job posting.
- Being unable to explain your own project in detail when asked follow-up questions.
- Undervaluing soft skills like communication and collaboration in technical interviews.
- Not researching the company's product or industry before the interview.

How to Choose a Target Role:
- Compare your current strongest skills against 3-4 role guides.
- Pick the role with the smallest skill gap first if you need to start earning experience quickly.
- It's normal to pivot roles early in a career — first job doesn't have to be a permanent choice.
